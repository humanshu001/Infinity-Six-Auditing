==========================================================================
40_MEVSandwich  --  MEV / cross-block sandwich on withdraw
==========================================================================

What this suite tests
---------------------
Whether an MEV bot can sandwich a user's withdraw() to make them mint
fewer i6 than expected. The system contract reads `getSpotPrice()` from
PancakeSwap reserves at the moment of withdraw and mints
`usdtReward * WAD / spotPrice` -- a higher spot price means a smaller
i6 mint to the user.

In-block sandwich is fully blocked by the same-block protections
(`lastBlockNumber` on system, `lastTxBlock`/`lastReceiveBlock` on
token). CROSS-BLOCK sandwich is what this suite measures.

How it tests it
---------------
Deploys a fresh i6 system seeded to spot ~ 1 USDT/i6, victim invests
1,000 USDT and matures 30 days. Three scenarios:

  1. Baseline: victim withdraws at clean spot.
  2. Cross-block sandwich: an MEV bot front-runs the victim's
     withdraw with a 20,000 USDT invest (MAX_INVESTMENT). Pancake's
     internal swap inside invest() pushes spot UP. The victim's
     withdraw in the next block mints against the higher spot price.
  3. Same-block defense: confirms the layered same-block locks.

Results
-------
[PASS] test_MEV_baseline_withdraw_amount
  - Baseline spot:                        1.001 USDT/i6
  - Baseline i6 minted to victim:         153.15 i6

[PASS] test_MEV_cross_block_sandwich_front_run_reduces_mint
  - MEV front-runs with 20,000 USDT invest.
  - Spot after the front-run:             1.025 USDT/i6
  - i6 minted to victim AFTER sandwich:   149.54 i6
  - Loss to victim:                       3.61 i6 (~2.35%)

[PASS] test_MEV_same_block_sandwich_is_blocked
  - Same-block defense layers active: system lastBlockNumber +
    token lastTxBlock + token lastReceiveBlock.

Plain-English summary
---------------------
In-block sandwich is impossible. Cross-block IS possible: an MEV bot
can invest right before a target withdraw (paying real USDT, not just
gas) and shrink the victim's i6 by ~2-3%. The MEV bot's cost is the
real invest amount; their incentive is murky because buying is closed
forever (they cannot easily resell the i6 they minted at low rates).
The most likely real-world manifestation is unintentional --
investments timed near withdraw clusters by independent EOAs cause
withdraw under-payment for everyone.

The fix neutralizes the attack entirely:
  Add `uint256 minTokensOut` argument to `withdraw()` and revert when
  `tokensToTransfer < minTokensOut`. The user (UI) computes the
  expected mint at submission time and sets minTokensOut to e.g.
  98% of expected -- any MEV front-run that pushes mint below 98%
  reverts.

-----------
